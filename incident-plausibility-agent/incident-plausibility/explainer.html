Incident Plausibility Agent

It checks whether a motor claim's story could really have happened where and when the claimant says, and whether the repair invoice fits that story. Every warning comes with evidence a human adjuster can verify.

How it works

Six steps, in order

Free outside data (no API key) LLM Plain code

  1. Locate the incidentAPI

    Uses the map pin from the claim form. Without one, it geocodes the typed address, which is less reliable because “Avenue Habib Bourguiba” exists in at least 5 places.

    Nominatim (OpenStreetMap)

  2. Gather the contextAPI

    Lists the roads, speed limits, traffic lights, roundabouts and car parks within 150 m, trying two mirror servers if the main one is busy. Gets the hourly weather plus sunrise and sunset for that date.

    Overpass (OpenStreetMap) · Open-Meteo

  3. Read the storyLLM · small, fast

    Turns the free text, in English, French or Arabic, into structured facts: what the car was doing, which side was hit, stated speed, weather, light, places, and any attempt to manipulate the AI. Every fact must quote the exact words that prove it.

  4. Verify the factscode

    Checks that each quote really is in the description. A fact without a real quote is dropped, so the LLM cannot add something the claimant never wrote.

  5. Reconstruct the incidentLLM · large × 3

    Works out the collision geometry, then predicts the impact zone, the parts that should be damaged, and any statements that contradict each other, quoted. It sees only the story. Three independent answers are compared, and the majority wins.

  6. Judge and scorecode

    Compares the facts with the map and weather, and the invoice with the reconstruction. Each warning sign gets points, a confidence level and a sentence of evidence. The AI interprets; code decides.

Prompt engineering

Why the LLM can't invent evidence

A fact the claimant never wrote would become a false fraud signal, so both prompts must back every claim with a quote, and code checks the quotes. Two of the guardrail tests:

Invented fact

Description: “It was a cold January morning. I was stopped at the traffic light…”

"stated_weather": "ice",
"stated_weather_quote": "the road was icy"

Quote not in the description → fact dropped → no false weather flag (0 pts).

Invented contradiction

All three answers report a contradiction with a quote the claimant never wrote.

"quote_a": "I was driving at 80 km/h",
"quote_b": "I was stopped at the traffic light"

Quote_a not found → contradiction ignored, with a note (0 pts).

  • Prompt chainingReading (step 3) and reasoning (step 5) are separate calls, so a failure can be traced to one step.
  • Evidence quoting, checked in codeEvery fact and contradiction carries the exact words that prove it.
  • Dynamic few-shotFrom 6 worked examples, the 2 closest to this claim are chosen, most similar last.
  • Hard negativesExamples teach that “cold morning” is not ice and “maybe around 8” is not a contradiction.
  • Reasoning first, strict JSONThe model explains the collision before answering; enums stop invented labels.
  • Self-consistencyThree answers, majority vote; their agreement becomes the confidence.
  • AbstentionA vague story gets “unknown”, and the invoice check is skipped instead of guessed.
  • Injection defence and detectionThe claimant's text is data; instructions inside it are reported as a fraud sign.
  • Provider-independent promptsRuns on free models (Groq, Gemini, Ollama). Every answer is checked against the schema in code, so formatting mistakes can't become fraud signals.

Prompts are versioned files (extraction v2 → v3, reconstruction v1 → v2). An evaluation runner compares old and new versions on 15 labeled claims in English, French and Arabic; it needs a free Groq or Gemini key to produce the before/after numbers.

Known limits

  • The weather history uses a 10–25 km grid and can miss a local shower, so weather signs are medium confidence only.
  • OpenStreetMap often lacks speed limits; a typical limit for the road type is used instead.
  • The public Overpass map servers are often overloaded; the agent tries three, then continues with fewer checks (status “partial”).
  • The reconstruction is a plausibility estimate, not a physics simulation.
  • Not yet tested: inside a running n8n instance, and with real LLM answers.
